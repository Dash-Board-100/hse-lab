The method

A dashboard is the starting point. Your reasoning is the skill.

Many HSE reports show numbers. Fewer help people decide what to do next. Every lab follows the same three moves.

01

Check the data

What does the number actually mean? Look at definitions, missing records, exclusions and the evidence behind a result.

02

Understand the risk

What deserves attention first? Find patterns, link them to controls, and note what the records cannot tell you.

03

Explain the next action

What should happen next? Write a short management brief with a finding, a limitation, an action and a decision.

Write the management brief

Example structure. In a real lab you write your own, then compare.

Finding
46 of 50 actions are closed. Effectiveness is verified for 12 of the 46.
Limitation
We cannot yet say whether the four similar events share the same hazard and failed control.
Next action
Review the repeated events against the original hazard, correction type and verification evidence.
Decision required
Agree an owner and clear criteria for verifying effectiveness.

Intended audience

For practitioners who turn HSE records into decisions

Who it supports

  • Early-career HSE staff: evidence of practical skills.
  • Practitioners: a method for checking data before presenting it.
  • Supervisors and managers: practice in explaining a decision.
  • Facilitators: a structured lab and worksheet for a sharing session.

Approach

  • Starts with a question, not a software menu.
  • Separates “action completed” from “effectiveness verified”.
  • Uses imperfect, clearly labelled dummy data.
  • Ends with a brief, not just another chart.
